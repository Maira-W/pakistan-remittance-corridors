# Memo: which corridors to serve first, and when to be ready

**For:** the product and treasury teams of a bank, exchange house or fintech that wants a share of remittances to Pakistan
**Evidence:** this repository. State Bank of Pakistan figures: annual by country to FY26, monthly by country from July 2019 to April 2024.

## Bottom line

Serve **Saudi Arabia, UAE, UK and USA** first: together they bring in 69% of the $41.6 billion Pakistan received in FY26. Add the next tier as you grow, and watch the fast risers (Italy (+19%), Australia (+37%) and Canada (+21%)). Be ready for the 21 days before each Eid, when remittances run about +11% above trend, and expect the UK, USA and Oman to show the clearest lift. The size of that lift is uncertain, so plan for a range.

## What the data show

**The flow is large and still growing.** $41.6 billion in FY26 is +8.6% on FY25. The dip after FY22 ($27.3 billion in FY23) has been more than recovered. July 2026 was +13% on July 2025.

**It is concentrated, but less than it was.** The Gulf states sent 65% of the money in FY20, 53% in FY23 and 54% in FY26: most of the fall came early, and the share has been steady since. Saudi Arabia (24%), the UAE (21%), the UK (15%) and the USA (9%) are the four large corridors. A standard concentration index across the 23 corridors fell from 0.17 to 0.14.

**Growth is not where the size is.** The FY26 gain of $3,286 million came mostly from UAE (+$978 million), Saudi Arabia (+$438 million) and UK (+$421 million). Outside the core, Italy (+19%), Australia (+37%) and Canada (+21%) grew fastest. The USA (-2.6%) and Oman (-1.7%) shrank, and Saudi Arabia grew only +4.7%.

**Money surges before Eid, modestly.** After removing the long-run trend, remittances run about +10.6% above normal in the 21 days before Eid (95% range -1.3% to +23.9%, p = 0.08). Eid al-Adha shows a clearer lift (+15%, p = 0.05) than Eid al-Fitr (+6%, p = 0.55). There is no sign of a dip afterwards (-4.1%, p = 0.29).

**The evidence is suggestive, not conclusive.** If the Eid dates are moved by the same number of days, 12% of the made-up calendars find a lift as large (13th largest of 97). With 46 usable months and only about nine Eids, the total cannot prove the effect.

**The lift is clearest for the diaspora corridors.** The UK (+23%, p = 0.010), the USA (+20%, p = 0.003) and Oman (+16%, p = 0.000) show a clear lift. Saudi Arabia (+7%) and the UAE (+8%) do not. 10 corridors were tested, so single results should be read with care.

**Some calendar months stand out.** March, April, June and July were strong months and January, May and December weak ones, measured per day so that short months do not look weak. Only three or four years sit behind each month. The strongest month averages +9% against trend and the weakest -12%.

## Recommendations

**1. Build for the four core corridors first.** Saudi Arabia, UAE, UK and USA carry 69% of the money. Price, speed and the receiving side (wallets, bank accounts, cash pick-up) matter most here, because small improvements apply to a large flow.

**2. Add the next tier and watch the risers.** Tier 2 (Italy, Oman, Qatar, Australia, Spain, Kuwait, Germany, Canada, France, Greece and Bahrain) holds 25% of the money. Among single countries, Italy (+19%), Australia (+37%) and Canada (+21%) are growing at least ten points faster than the total. Check them against your own pipeline before committing.

**3. Do not depend on the Gulf alone.** It still sends 54% of the money, down from 65% in FY20. In FY26 it grew +7.8% against +8.6% overall, while Europe grew +11% and Asia-Pacific +24%; North America was flat (+0.9%).

**4. Plan liquidity and campaigns around Eid.** Hold extra capacity for the 21 days before each Eid. For the next two Eids (Eid al-Fitr 2027 and Eid al-Adha 2027), the indicative extra flow in the busiest month is about $275 million at the central estimate and up to $613 million at the top of the range (table below). Focus UK, USA and Oman marketing on that window.

**5. Use the quiet months.** January, May and December are the weakest months, a natural time for onboarding drives and system changes.

**6. Measure your own flows.** These are national totals. A provider should repeat the analysis on its own transactions, where it can also see customers, prices and channels.

## What this analysis cannot tell you

- **Individual providers.** It describes the whole country, not one company's customers.
- **Informal channels.** Only remittances recorded by the central bank are counted.
- **Prices.** Exchange rates and fees are not modelled.
- **Recent months by country.** Monthly country data end in April 2024.
- **Cause.** Associations only: 2020-03: COVID-19 lockdowns; 2020-09: Roshan Digital Account transfers counted; 2023-07: Exchange-rate reforms and a crackdown on informal channels.
